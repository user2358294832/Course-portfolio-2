# Podcast: Open-Back vs Closed-Back Headphones

Bu podcast bölümünde, kulaklık dünyasının en temel ayrımı olan **Açık Arka (Open-Back)** ve **Kapalı Arka (Closed-Back)** tasarımlarını masaya yatırıyorum. 

Consumer (tüketici) odaklı ANC (Aktif Gürültü Engelleme) özellikli kulaklıkların neden audiophile'ler tarafından her zaman tercih edilmediğini, **HRTF**, **Acoustic Impedance**, **Soundstage** ve **Imaging** gibi teknik kavramlar üzerinden, kendi sesimden yaklaşık 10 dakikalık bir anlatımla açıklıyorum.

"These days we see people spending lots of money for headphones. Back in the days, it was seen pretty abnormal to spend 400$ for a pair of headphones, but if you want to get the latest pair of ANC cans, you have to spend at least that amount of money. 

Now, this amount of money may feel overwhelming because you are buying these headphones to enjoy your music, listen to podcasts like you are doing right now, and watch some videos, films, etc. 

Considering these areas, you have to have something that sounds good -to your ears-, something that feels comfy for long listening sessions, and you say the rest. 

But did you know that most of the audiophiles don't spend their money on these type of headphones? Well, today, I will be talking about why most popular headphones suck compared to their open-backed family members. Let's begin.

Now, we have two types of headphones today: closed-backs and open-backs. 

In which ways do these differ? Well, before we get into technical stuff, I want to clear out the basics. 

Closed-back headphones are not hard to imagine. Basically think of the headphones you are using or seeing on majority of people's heads right now. All of them are closed-back. Both of their sides are sealed with plastic or metal. They don't leak sound, they allow active noice cancellation, ANC for short. 

But what they fail in is the soundstage, imaging, sound quality (this is of course subjective), and HRTF, I will get to that in a minute. 

Because they are closed system, dynamic drivers in those headphones cannot move freely. That's why, producers tend to make them a bit wider from the sides. 

Because they cannot send the sound outwards, those frequencies directly jump back to your ears, that's why bass guitar and drums slam in your ears, they feel punchy and explosive. Well, bass heads enjoy these type of headphones. 

Additionally, there is a thing that is called HRTF, which stands for Head-Related Transfer Function. 

Now this basically means that the sound you get from a headphone differs a lot based pn the placement of the pads, by your headshape, your ears, etc. 

That's why most of the people hears a headphone different than the others. 

In this point, there is a thing that we say Acoustic Impedance. 

Think of a person trying to move freely in a place, or a room full of air, only fresh air. In this context, that person can freely move left-right. This is an example of low impedance in real life. 

Now think about a person trying to move freely in a place full of water. They cannot move freely, right? Because of the pressure. This is an example of high impedance in real life. 

Think about this in headphones, if you have high acoustic impedance, sound cannot move freely in the headphones. Air can't pass through. And our ears don't produce natural sound like that. Because of this, perception of the sound of a particular pair of headphones changes from person to person. 

Now let's talk about open back headphones. 

First let's imagine an open backed headphone. 

Think about those Apple's Airpods Maxes. They have that sleek design, premium feeling headband, chasis, cushions, etc. Now put grills on those rectangular sides. It looks awful right, hahaha, I know. This was a bad way to imagine an open backed headphone. 

Okay, let's try another thing. Now, I want you to open you browser, safari, chrome, Firefox... Anything's fine. Now search: HiFiMAN Sundara.

You see that sleek, full black design, right? That cool headband, chasis. This is what an open backed headphone looks like. 

Now without closing your browser, now search for Sennheiser HD 600. Writing HD 600 will do the work. You see that massive ear cushions with those grills, right? Now, this allows for most of the things. 

Firstly, you get better soundstage. Unlike closed back pairs, because the sound can travel freely in the place, you will feel like you are in a room full of speakers. That quarantine effect coming from closed backs will fly away while you're wearing these. 

Secondly, imaging is improved. Now you can pinpoint where is the bass guitar, where are the vocals, where are other instruments like sax, violin, piano, etc. 

Finally, natural sound and better HRTF. Because sound can better travel, our ears produce those waves more naturally. Thus, you don't have to fear for the HRTF. 

So, are open-backed headphones perfect? Of course not. There are some trade-offs. 

They may not deliver enough bass for most people and feel analytical in sound. Due to the open backed design, these type of headphones generally tend to suffer from lack of that punchy, explosive, or pleasant bass for some people. Most people like to enjoy the ‘coloured’ sound of closed-backs. Generally, some open-backs tend to reveal the flaws of the music with their flat or treble-heavy tuning. That's why while good mixed songs will sound stunning, poorly-mixed ones will suffer from these headphones for sure. 

Another downside for open backs is that they leak sound. Now, to be honest, these headphones are not designed to be used on the streets, when you go to the gym, or when you are at your workplace. They are for personal environments where you are generally alone and can focus and enjoy your music. 

Because they are open backed, they will both leak sound and let in sound. So if you're on a public transportation, you will likely to give concert to other passengers, or you are going to hear them gossiping about you. 

That's why, most of the people spend their money on closed-back ones since they have ANC to block out external noise and can offer less sound leakage. 
So why audiophiles normally pick open-backed headphones over closed-backed ones? 

Well, the answer is simple: because you can get a better sound quality, clarity, massive soundstages, pinpoint imaging and much more flat but at the same time uncoloured, enjoyful sound for the half of the price of those expensive ANC cans. 

Now, this doesn't mean that open-backed headphones are the best. Each headphone type suits you based on your needs. If you live in a noisy environment, you may want to get a closed-backed headphone, or if you commute to work, go to the gym frequently, or to put it simply: If you want convenience, closed-back headphones are your comrades. 

On the other hand, if you want to spend more money, enter to this hobby, get more clarity, soundstage, imaging, better tonal balance, and much more natural sound, and if the environment is nice, open-backed headphones will give you lifelong pleasure. 

Finally, this is important to note that closed back headphones are generally for consumers. This means that, they offer consumer oriented sound profile that we generally call V-shape. In this profile you most of the time get an increased bass and treble, and recessed mids. And these headphones are easy to drive with their low impedance numbers like 32ohms. 

On the contrary, open-backed headphones generally have a more studio sound. They are mostly flat, no part of music stand forward or backward based on the tuning of the headphones. And they are harder to drive. Some have small numbers like 25 ohms or 32 ohms. Or others have quite large numbers like 300 ohms.

Thank you for joining me on this podcast today. I hope you had an enjoyable session and learned new things about the audio world. Just know that everything is best based on your budget, preferences and where will you use your product. Take care!"
